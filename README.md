## 🔍 What ScamLens Looks For

ScamLens is built around the patterns that real scams repeat again and again. When it reads your message, it is checking for things such as:

- **Urgency and pressure** — "act within 24 hours," "your account will be closed," "final notice."
- **Authority impersonation** — messages pretending to come from a bank, delivery company, government office, or well-known tech brand.
- **Suspicious links** — web addresses that imitate a real company but are slightly misspelled or use an unusual ending.
- **Requests for sensitive information** — passwords, one-time codes, card numbers, ID photos.
- **Unusual payment requests** — gift cards, crypto transfers, wire transfers, or payment apps that offer no protection.
- **Too-good-to-be-true offers** — prizes you never entered for, jobs that pay far above market rate, investment returns that sound impossible.
- **Grammar and formatting oddities** — unusual phrasing, mismatched logos, generic greetings like "Dear Customer."
- **Contact redirection** — being pushed off the official platform onto WhatsApp, Telegram, or a personal phone number.

Each of these is shown to you with a short explanation, so you know exactly what made ScamLens concerned.

ScamLens combines a few different technologies behind a single simple screen:

2. **Language analysis (NLP)** — the text is then examined for the persuasion tactics and red flags described above.
3. **Risk scoring** — the findings are combined into a single risk level, from low to high.
4. **Explanation** — finally, ScamLens writes out its reasoning in everyday language, so the result is something you can actually understand and learn from.

## 💡 Safety Tips While You Wait

ScamLens is a helpful second opinion, but these habits will protect you in any situation:

- If a message creates panic, slow down. Urgency is the scammer's main tool.
- Never share a one-time verification code with anyone, even someone claiming to be from your bank.
- Do not click links in unexpected messages. Open the company's official website yourself instead.
- Confirm any payment request through a second channel — call the person or organization using a number you already trust.
- If you have already shared information, contact your bank immediately and change your passwords.
